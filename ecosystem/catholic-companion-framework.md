# Catholic Companion Framework for Technology and Innovation

---

## 1. Executive Summary & Provenance

Created in 2025 by **Meaghan Crowley-Sullivan, M.Ed., M.A.**, the **[Catholic Companion Framework for Technology and Innovation](https://www.lmu.edu/ideal)** is a comprehensive pedagogical and theological framework designed for Catholic educators navigating artificial intelligence, emerging technologies, and digital innovation. 

The framework holds unique institutional significance for the Saint Francis High School AI Initiative:
* **Alumna Authorship & Local Grounding**: Meaghan Crowley-Sullivan is an alumna of Saint Francis High School and a frontline educator.
* **Institutional Bridge**: Crowley-Sullivan works concurrently as a practitioner with the **Loyola Marymount University (LMU) iDEAL Institute** (an authorized professional development partner for the framework) and the **University of Notre Dame's DELTA Framework** team.
* **Foundational Working Tool**: Saint Francis High School faculty worked extensively with the excerpted Catholic Companion Framework alongside foundational Holy Cross documents prior to the formal rollout of the DELTA Framework.

The Catholic Companion Framework moves beyond narrow acceptable-use policies or commercial AI literacy standards. It offers a vision of **Digital Discipleship**: forming young people who possess not only computational agility, but the moral conscience, communal solidarity, and theological grounding needed to place technology at the service of humanity and creation.

### Strand 1: Imago Dei (Created in God's Image)
In a culture where foundation models simulate reasoning, fluent conversation, and creative artistry, students are invited to examine what makes humanity truly sacred. *Imago Dei* anchors education in the truth that every person bears the divine image and is uniquely capable of genuine love, moral contemplation, and self-gift—capacities no algorithm can possess. Dignity is treated as an unconditional gift, countering the technocratic habit of valuing individuals by their computational efficiency.

### Strand 2: Co-Creation & Stewardship
Students are surrounded by generative engines promising instantaneous creation. Co-Creation & Stewardship teaches that **"to create is also to care."** This strand directly grounds technological design in reverence for the Creator and creation itself:
* Reminds students that their digital designs, software architectures, and computational queries touch both human communities and the **earth that sustains them**.
* Provides a natural classroom bridge to Pope Francis's *[Laudato Si’](/ecosystem/laudato-si.md)* and Saint Francis High School's [Sustainability Framework](/references/st-francis-sustainability.md).

### Strand 3: Moral Conscience & Discernment
Rather than reducing digital ethics to a list of forbidden behaviors (an [Ethical Floor vs. Moral Ceiling](/concepts/ethical-floor-moral-ceiling.md)), this strand forms students to cultivate **Phronesis (Practical Wisdom)**. Conscience is understood not as a subjective feeling or an external rulebook, but as a living dialogue with God that calls learners to pause, evaluate algorithmic suggestions, and choose the good with integrity.

### Strand 4: Communion & Solidarity
Digital platforms often foster envy, outrage, and atomization. Communion & Solidarity challenges adolescents to consider who is marginalized or harmed by automated systems. Students examine algorithmic bias, digital divides, and data privacy, learning to employ technology as an instrument of reconciliation and community rather than exploitation.

### Strand 5: Wonder, Mystery, & Awe
In an ecosystem dominated by notifications, instant answers, and friction-free generation, students rarely encounter the silence that births original insight. This strand nurtures humility before the unsearchable mystery of God. Students learn that wisdom begins with wonder, and that the deepest human truths—love, sacrifice, faith, aesthetic beauty—transcend computational optimization.

## 5. Strategic Implications for Saint Francis High School

1. **Classroom Lesson Design**: The framework's five strands provide teachers across all disciplines with concrete formative reflection questions to include on assignment sheets and syllabus contracts.
2. **K-12 Developmental Scaffolding**: Teachers can reference Crowley-Sullivan's grade-band substrands to ensure high school students progress from basic user ethics into authentic digital discipleship.
3. **Bridge between Faith and Tech**: Validates St. Francis High School's distinctive mission—demonstrating that faith and STEM innovation are partners in human flourishing.
