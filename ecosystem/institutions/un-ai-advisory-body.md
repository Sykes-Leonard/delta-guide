# United Nations High-Level Advisory Body on AI: Institutional Profile, Governance & Research Agenda

---

## 1. Executive Summary & Institutional Charter

Convened in October 2023 by United Nations Secretary-General **António Guterres**, the **[United Nations High-Level Advisory Body on Artificial Intelligence](https://www.un.org/en/ai-advisory-body)** brought together 39 eminent experts from government, industry, academia, and civil society across 33 nations to address the critical lack of coordinated international AI governance.

* **Mission & Scope**: The Advisory Body was tasked with formulating actionable institutional blueprints to overcome the **"global governance deficit"** in AI. The body insists that AI must be governed as a **global public good** anchored in the UN Charter, the Universal Declaration of Human Rights, and the 2030 Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs), ensuring that frontier AI benefits all of humanity rather than reinforcing geopolitical and economic monopolies.
* **Genesis & Affiliation**: Established within the Executive Office of the Secretary-General at the United Nations Headquarters in New York, the body conducted 18 deep-dive thematic sessions, over 50 regional roundtables, and processed more than 250 formal institutional submissions.
* **Key Leadership & Fellows**: Co-chaired by **Carme Artigas** (former Secretary of State for Digitalization and AI, Spain) and **James Manyika** (President of Research, Labs, Technology & Society at Google-Alphabet / DeepMind), and comprising 39 global leaders, including Ranil Jayawardena, Alondra Nelson, and Amandeep Singh Gill (UN Tech Envoy).

## 2. Institutional Architecture & Typology

```
Typology:         Global Multilateral Authority & Policy Advisory Body
Governance Model: United Nations Secretary-General Advisory Panel
Target Horizon:   Global Governance, Human Rights, Equity & Transnational AI Architecture
```

### Core Research & Policy Pillars:
* **Pillar 1: Shared Scientific Understanding**: Establishing an IPCC-style International Scientific Panel on AI to issue authoritative annual consensus reports on capability frontiers, safety benchmarks, and catastrophic risks.
* **Pillar 2: Global Policy Interoperability & Standards Harmonization**: Convening twice-yearly ministerial dialogues and creating an AI Standards Exchange to harmonize testing methodologies across sovereign borders.
* **Pillar 3: Distributive Equity & Compute Commons**: Creating a Global Fund for AI and Capacity Development Network to provide public compute, open training sets, and institutional training to the Global South.

* **Architecture & Standards Interoperability**: Enterprise and educational architectures should align data schemas with emerging ISO/IEC and IEEE standards tracked by the UN Standards Exchange.
