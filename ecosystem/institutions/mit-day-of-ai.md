# MIT RAISE: Day of AI Curriculum & K-12 AI Literacy

---

## 1. Executive Summary & Institutional Mission

**MIT RAISE** (Responsible AI for Social Empowerment and Education) is an interdisciplinary initiative across the MIT Media Lab, Schwarzman College of Computing, and Open Learning, directed by Professor **Cynthia Breazeal** (Dean for Digital Learning). In collaboration with educators and researchers worldwide, MIT RAISE developed **[Day of AI](https://www.dayofai.org)**, a global, open-access curriculum initiative designed to introduce K-12 students and educators to foundational artificial intelligence concepts, capabilities, societal impacts, and ethical considerations.

While higher-education initiatives often focus on academic integrity and graduate-level training, MIT RAISE addresses the critical developmental window of primary and secondary education. The initiative asserts that before students can responsibly navigate or ethically critique generative AI, they must first achieve **fundamental AI awareness**—understanding that AI systems are not magical, omniscient beings, but mathematical systems trained on human data that reflect human priorities, limitations, and biases.

Importantly, Day of AI emphasizes a **human-centered, agency-affirming pedagogy**: students do not merely learn *about* AI; they learn how to interrogate algorithms, design responsible applications, and evaluate the societal and physical impacts of computational systems—including their environmental footprint.

### Core Curricular Bands & Themes:
* **Elementary (Grades K–5)**: Foundations of machine learning, classification, voice assistants, and understanding the difference between biological life and programmed machines.
* **Middle School (Grades 6–8)**: Teachable machines, generative imagery, recommendation algorithms, algorithmic bias, and digital citizenship.
* **High School (Grades 9–12)**: Large language models, deepfakes, intellectual property, ethical decision-making frameworks, workforce implications, and the **environmental and climate impacts of AI compute**.

### 3.1 Foundational "AI Awareness"
The starting premise of MIT Day of AI is that **AI literacy is a baseline civic competency**. Without foundational literacy, students oscillate between two harmful extremes:
1. **Technological Mystification**: Attributing supernatural intelligence, empathy, and absolute authority to chatbot responses.
2. **Cynical Exploitation**: Using AI as a black-box cheat engine to bypass cognitive effort.

Day of AI grounds students in how transformers and generative models operate: training corpora, parameter weights, probability distributions, and the reality of hallucination. This transparency demystifies the tool and restores student agency.

### 3.2 Environmental & Physical Substrate of AI
A unique and vital contribution of MIT RAISE’s high school curriculum is the explicit examination of **AI and Climate Change**. 

Rather than treating AI as an ethereal "cloud" software, Day of AI walks students through:
* **The Energy Grid**: The massive electrical power required to train frontier models and respond to billions of inference queries.
* **Datacenter Resources**: Water consumption for cooling server racks and land use impacts.
* **Hardware Extraction**: The mineral and human supply chains (lithium, cobalt, rare earth elements) required to build AI hardware.

This curriculum module serves as the primary secular bridge to Catholic Social Teaching's call for [Integral Ecology](/ecosystem/laudato-si.md), demonstrating empirically to students that *every digital prompt has a physical, planetary cost*.

## 5. Strategic Integration with St. Francis High School Blueprint

For the St. Francis High School AI Initiative:

1. **Curricular Scaffolding for "AI Awareness"**: Day of AI provides turnkey modular units that can be inserted into freshman theology, computer science, and biology classes to establish common vocabulary and baseline awareness before students enter higher-level augmented courses.
2. **Bridging Dignity and the Earth**: Day of AI’s environmental modules directly reinforce the DELTA extension where **Dignity includes the earth**, linking secular environmental data with Pope Francis's *Laudato Si’*.
3. **Teacher Professional Development**: Enables teachers across non-STEM disciplines (Humanities, Arts, Social Studies) to comfortably facilitate discussions on AI without needing advanced technical backgrounds.
