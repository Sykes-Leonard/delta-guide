# Institute for Human Flourishing: Institutional Profile, Governance & Research Agenda

---

## 1. Executive Summary & Institutional Charter

Launched in September 2026 as a major inter-regional initiative backed by **The Rockefeller Foundation** and **Schmidt Philanthropies**, the **[Institute for Human Flourishing (IHF)](https://flourishinginstitute.org/)** is an action-policy research lab focused exclusively on ensuring that artificial intelligence expands agency, economic dignity, and self-determination for historically marginalized communities in the **Global South**.

* **Mission & Scope**: Headquartered in New Delhi, India, with regional hubs in Nairobi, Kenya and São Paulo, Brazil, the IHF operates on the principle that the current global AI paradigm is excessively oriented toward automation in high-income economies. IHF pioneers the theory and deployment of **"labor-using AI"**—technological architectures specifically engineered to increase the productivity, wages, and decision-making authority of smallholder farmers, community health workers, and informal laborers.
* **Genesis & Affiliation**: Co-funded with an initial $50 million catalytic commitment from philanthropic foundations, IHF collaborates directly with grassroots non-governmental organizations, regional universities, and national planning ministries across Asia, Africa, and Latin America.
* **Key Leadership & Fellows**: Led by an inter-regional council of development economists, rural sociologists, and machine learning practitioners from the Global South.

## 2. Institutional Architecture & Typology

```
Typology:         Action-Policy Research Lab & Global South Development Institute
Governance Model: Independent Philanthropic Initiative (Rockefeller / Schmidt Partnership)
Target Horizon:   Frontline Worker Agency, Labor-Using AI & Agrarian Data Sovereignty
```

### Core Research & Policy Pillars:
* **Pillar 1: "Labor-Using" vs. "Labor-Saving" AI**: Reframing technical objectives to develop tools that create new tasks and amplify human labor rather than replacing human workers with autonomous agents.
* **Pillar 2: Agrarian & Community Health Augmentation**: Deploying localized, voice-enabled, low-latency models to empower frontline workers in low-connectivity rural environments.
* **Pillar 3: Data Dignity & Indigenous Data Sovereignty**: Creating community-owned data trusts that prevent multinational tech conglomerates from extracting localized agricultural, medical, and linguistic data without fair equity returns.

## 6. Strategic Implications for System Architecture & Team Operations

* **Optimize for Low-Bandwidth Edge Execution**: Ensure educational and operational software is capable of graceful degradation, local caching, and offline inference for users with intermittent network access.
* **Support Audio and Multimodal Interfaces**: When designing community-facing tools, integrate local speech-to-text models that accommodate varied literacy levels and regional accents.
* **Community Boundary Review (CSH)**: In every system deployment, execute Critical Systems Heuristics reviews to verify who truly benefits from the data collection and whether local user sovereignty is respected.
