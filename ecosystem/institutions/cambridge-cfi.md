# Leverhulme Centre for the Future of Intelligence: Institutional Profile, Governance & Research Agenda

---

## 1. Executive Summary & Institutional Charter

Based at the University of Cambridge and operating in partnership with the University of Oxford, Imperial College London, and UC Berkeley, the **[Leverhulme Centre for the Future of Intelligence (CFI)](https://www.lcfi.ac.uk/)** is a global interdisciplinary research center dedicated to investigating the opportunities, ethical challenges, and existential implications of artificial intelligence over long civilizational horizons.

* **Mission & Scope**: Founded on the conviction that the creation of artificial intelligence may be the biggest event in human history, CFI’s mission is to ensure that AI is developed to support human flourishing, democratic resilience, and ecological stability. The Centre challenges narrow techno-solutionism, analyzing how historical narratives, power dynamics, and cultural assumptions shape technical trajectories.
* **Genesis & Affiliation**: Established in 2016 through a £10 million grant from the Leverhulme Trust, CFI connects philosophers, computer scientists, anthropologists, legal theorists, and cognitive scientists across the Cambridge ecosystem.
* **Key Leadership & Fellows**: Led by **Prof. Stephen Cave** (Executive Director; author of the DeepMind Institute's foundational Essay 7 on utopianism), co-founded by philosopher **Prof. Huw Price** and astrophysicist **Lord Martin Rees** (former President of the Royal Society).

### Core Research & Policy Pillars:
* **Pillar 1: Pragmatic Utopianism**: Formulating visions of a positive AI future grounded in five ethical axioms: humility, pluralism, reversibility, attentiveness to vulnerability, and radical hope.
* **Pillar 2: AI Narratives & Sociotechnical Imaginaries**: Deconstructing cultural myths, media depictions, and racial/gender biases embedded in technological discourse to promote inclusive alternatives.
* **Pillar 3: The Philosophy of Non-Human Intelligence**: Investigating the boundaries of machine consciousness, moral status, and cognitive diversity across artificial, human, and animal minds.

## 6. Strategic Implications for System Architecture & Team Operations

* **Enforce Design Reversibility**: Workflows and administrative automations must be architected so that automated policies can be rolled back or adjusted without breaking core operations.
* **Challenge Narrative Determinism**: When educating staff and students, reject the narrative that total automation is an "inevitable future"; emphasize human policy agency in choosing when and where to adopt AI.
* **Vulnerability Auditing**: Before deploying any algorithmic tool, conduct an explicit assessment of how the system impacts the least technically proficient and most vulnerable stakeholders.
