# Carnegie Mellon University: The Simon Initiative, GAITAR & Learning Engineering

---

## 1. Executive Summary & Institutional Charter

At **Carnegie Mellon University (CMU)** in Pittsburgh, Pennsylvania, the intersection of artificial intelligence and education is anchored in the legacy of Nobel Laureate **Herbert A. Simon**, who co-founded cognitive psychology and artificial intelligence. Rather than adopting ungrounded enthusiasm or broad prohibitions, CMU approaches AI through **learning engineering**—an empirical, data-driven discipline that applies cognitive science to design, test, and iterate educational interventions.

* **Mission & Scope**: Led by the **[Simon Initiative](https://www.cmu.edu/simon/)** and the **[Group for Research on AI in the Classroom (GAITAR)](https://www.cmu.edu/eberly/ai-classroom/)** at the Eberly Center for Teaching Excellence and Educational Innovation, CMU investigates how generative AI affects cognitive load, working memory, conceptual scaffolding, and authentic skill acquisition.
* **Genesis & Affiliation**: Spanning CMU’s School of Computer Science, Dietrich College of Humanities and Social Sciences, and the Human-Computer Interaction Institute (HCII), the Initiative treats every classroom as a living, measurable cognitive laboratory.
* **The "Guidance Paradox" Breakthrough**: CMU’s empirical studies reveal a critical pedagogical paradox: unconstrained student access to LLMs can speed up task completion by up to 40% while simultaneously degrading long-term conceptual retention and problem-solving transfer. However, when AI is constrained by structured cognitive scaffolding, it enhances deep learning.

Target Horizon:   Cognitive Science of Learning, Instructional Scaffolding & Empirical EdTech

### Core Research & Policy Pillars:
* **Pillar 1: Learning Engineering & Cognitive Scaffolding**: Applying cognitive models to sequence student tasks, ensuring that AI assistance fades as student competence grows (faded scaffolding).
* **Pillar 2: GAITAR Empirical Classroom Research**: Conducting rigorous, controlled studies across computer science, engineering, and humanities courses measuring actual learning gains versus synthetic performance.
* **Pillar 3: AI-Augmented Tutoring with Socratic Scaffolding**: Developing and auditing intelligent tutoring systems (CTAT/Open Learning Initiative) that guide students through problem schemas without solving problems for them.

## 6. Strategic Implications for System Architecture & High School AI Initiatives

For secondary school programs (e.g. St. Francis High School) and educational platform builders:

1. **Implement Faded Scaffolding**: Software interfaces should adjust assistance dynamically based on student mastery—providing strong hints and breakdown steps for novice learners, but gradually fading out AI assistance as students advance toward independent mastery.
2. **Collect and Analyze Process Telemetry**: Rather than inspecting only the final student submission, analyze process logs to identify when students become stuck or when they overly rely on synthetic suggestions.
3. **Reject Unconstrained Chat Interfaces**: Do not give secondary students open, unfiltered conversational chatbots for homework; wrap model endpoints in structured scaffolding workflows.
