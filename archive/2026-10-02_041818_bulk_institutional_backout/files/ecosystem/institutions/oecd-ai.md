# OECD.AI Policy Observatory: Institutional Profile, Governance & Research Agenda

---

## 1. Executive Summary & Institutional Charter

Operating within the Directorate for Science, Technology and Innovation of the **Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development (OECD)** in Paris, France, **[OECD.AI](https://oecd.ai/)** is the world’s foremost intergovernmental clearinghouse for tracking, analyzing, and shaping public policies on artificial intelligence.

* **Mission & Scope**: OECD.AI’s mandate is to foster trustworthy AI that respects human rights and democratic values while driving sustainable economic growth. Originally promulgating the foundational *OECD AI Principles* in May 2019 (the basis for the G20 AI Principles), the OECD substantially updated its recommendations in **May 2024** to address general-purpose and generative AI, centering **human well-being and inclusive growth** as the primary criteria for evaluating technical progress.
* **Genesis & Affiliation**: Supported by 38 OECD member countries and 9 non-member adhering nations (representing 47 sovereign jurisdictions, including the US, EU, UK, Japan, and Canada), the Observatory bridges the G7, G20, and Global Partnership on AI (GPAI).
* **Key Leadership & Fellows**: Led by **Karine Perset** (Head of the AI Unit, OECD) and overseen by the OECD Committee on Digital Economy Policy (CDEP).

## 2. Institutional Architecture & Typology

```
Typology:         Intergovernmental Policy Observatory & Economic Standards Body
Governance Model: Multilateral Organization (38 OECD Members + 9 Adhering Nations)
Target Horizon:   Inclusive Growth, Sustainable Development, Well-Being & Policy Alignment
```

### Core Research & Policy Pillars:
* **Pillar 1: Well-Being and Inclusive Growth**: Elevating Principle 1.1 to mandate that AI systems must proactively augment human capabilities, reduce socioeconomic inequalities, and support environmental sustainability.
* **Pillar 2: Global Policy Observatory & Live Tracking**: Maintaining a real-time global database of over 1,000 national AI policies, investment strategies, and statutory initiatives across more than 70 nations.
* **Pillar 3: Trustworthy AI Benchmarks & Metrics**: Formulating standardized definitions of AI actors, foundation models, risk classification methodologies, and transparency criteria to prevent regulatory fragmentation.

## 6. Strategic Implications for System Architecture & Team Operations

* **Align with OECD Incident Reporting Taxonomy**: Internal systems monitoring should align error logging with the OECD AI Incident framework to systematically track algorithmic anomalies and bias.
* **Prioritize Job Quality and Teacher Well-Being**: When automating administrative workflows in education, measure whether the tool actually relieves educator stress or simply increases administrative quotas and screen dependency.
* **Ensure Multi-Jurisdiction Interoperability**: Software components should be designed to comply with OECD-harmonized standards, simplifying compliance across transatlantic regulatory frameworks.
