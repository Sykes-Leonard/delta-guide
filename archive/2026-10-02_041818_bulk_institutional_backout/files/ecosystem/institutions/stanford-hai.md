# Stanford Institute for Human-Centered Artificial Intelligence: Institutional Profile, Governance & Research Agenda

---

## 1. Executive Summary & Institutional Charter

Established in 2019 at Stanford University in Silicon Valley, the **[Stanford Institute for Human-Centered Artificial Intelligence (HAI)](https://hai.stanford.edu/)** was founded to guide and build the future of artificial intelligence with human needs, human capabilities, and societal well-being placed squarely at the center of development.

* **Mission & Scope**: Stanford HAI is dedicated to advancing AI research, education, policy, and practice to enhance human conditions. Its core organizing thesis rejects pure labor substitution and autonomous displacement, advocating instead for **human augmentation**—engineering computational systems designed to collaborate with, amplify, and empower human workers, educators, clinicians, and citizens.
* **Genesis & Affiliation**: Housed across Stanford University’s seven schools (spanning engineering, humanities, medicine, law, business, education, and sustainability), HAI bridges technical frontier engineering with rigorous sociotechnical, legal, and humanist oversight.
* **Key Leadership & Fellows**: Co-directed by pioneering computer vision scientist **Dr. Fei-Fei Li** (Denning Family Co-Director) and philosopher **Dr. John Etchemendy** (Denning Family Co-Director; former Stanford University Provost). Major initiatives include the **Center for Research on Foundation Models (CRFM)**, directed by **Dr. Percy Liang**.

### Core Research & Policy Pillars:
* **Pillar 1: Human Augmentation over Substitution**: Developing collaborative intelligence models where algorithms perform perceptual or cognitive sub-tasks while leaving strategic intent, moral judgment, and creative leadership to humans.
* **Pillar 2: Foundation Model Transparency & Benchmarking**: Auditing commercial and open-weights models through the *Center for Research on Foundation Models (CRFM)*, establishing empirical baselines for bias, copyright infringement, toxicity, and reasoning depth.
* **Pillar 3: AI in Education & Cognitive Agency**: Advancing human-centered learning through the **Stanford Accelerator for Learning**, developing the CRAFT curriculum (Classroom-Ready Resources About AI for Teaching), and pioneering the shift from grading "ghostwritten" final products to harvesting real-time "process data."

## 6. Strategic Implications for System Architecture & Team Operations

* **Adopt HELM Evaluation Standards**: When selecting and fine-tuning models for educational and administrative tasks, benchmark performance against HELM multi-metric criteria (robustness, fairness, calibration) rather than single-metric accuracy.
* **Engineer for Collaboration over Full Autonomy**: User workflows must be structured as collaborative dialogues where the human remains the author and reviewer, preventing passive acceptance of model-generated text.
* **Pedagogical Safeguards Against Atrophy**: In school assignments, deploy Stanford-recommended scaffolding patterns (e.g., iterative prompting, counter-argument exploration) rather than end-to-end task completion engines.
