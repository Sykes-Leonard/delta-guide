# The Russell Group: Joint Principles on Generative AI in Higher Education

---

## 1. Executive Summary & Institutional Charter

Representing 24 of the United Kingdom's leading research-intensive universities—including **University of Oxford**, **University of Cambridge**, **Imperial College London**, **University College London (UCL)**, and **University of Edinburgh**—the **[Russell Group](https://russellgroup.ac.uk/)** established the world’s first coordinated, multi-institutional national framework governing generative AI in higher education.

* **Mission & Scope**: Rather than issuing fragmented, course-by-course bans or leaving individual departments to craft ad-hoc rules, the Russell Group vice-chancellors and academic leads developed a collective, principles-based compact. The framework commits member institutions to actively train students and staff in AI literacy, adapt assessment formats to maintain academic rigor, and ensure equitable access to technology.
* **The Transition from Prohibition to Literacy**: The Russell Group’s pivotal contribution was moving the global higher-education sector past the initial panic of "banning AI" toward a sustainable model of **institutional integration**. The group recognized that uncritical bans are technically unfeasible, widen socioeconomic divides, and fail to prepare graduates for an AI-integrated workforce.

### The Five Core Principles of the Russell Group Accord:
1. **Principle 1: Fostering Student & Staff AI Literacy**: Universities will support students and staff to become AI-literate, understanding how generative AI operates, its limitations, ethical risks, and appropriate applications.
2. **Principle 2: Equipping Staff to Guide Student Use**: Institutions will invest in professional development for faculty and teaching assistants, enabling them to adapt course design and provide pedagogically grounded AI guidance.
3. **Principle 3: Adapting Teaching & Assessment**: Member institutions will continuously adapt teaching methodologies and examination formats to evaluate authentic student learning and incorporate ethical AI use.
4. **Principle 4: Upholding Academic Rigour & Integrity**: Universities will maintain transparent academic conduct standards while ensuring that academic integrity policies reflect technological realities.
5. **Principle 5: Collaborative Best Practice Sharing**: The 24 universities commit to pooling empirical data, course evaluations, and policy reviews to adapt dynamically as AI capabilities evolve.

## 6. Strategic Implications for System Architecture & High School AI Initiatives

For educational software development and secondary school programs (e.g. St. Francis High School):

1. **Adopt Consortium-Wide Baseline Principles**: Establish school-wide consensus rather than fragmented, conflicting classroom policies across different departments.
2. **Mandate Institutional AI Literacy Curricula**: Formally teach students how to prompt, verify, critique, and document AI interactions as a core graduation requirement.
3. **Enterprise Equity Tiering**: Provide all students with standardized access to enterprise-grade AI tools with data protection and privacy guardrails, preventing disparities caused by personal subscription access.
