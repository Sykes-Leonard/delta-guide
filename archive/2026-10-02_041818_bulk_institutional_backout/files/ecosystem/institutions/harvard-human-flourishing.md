# Harvard Human Flourishing Program: Institutional Profile, Governance & Research Agenda

---

## 1. Executive Summary & Institutional Charter

Founded within the Institute for Quantitative Social Science (IQSS) at Harvard University and directed by epidemiologist and biostatistician **Prof. Tyler J. VanderWeele**, the **[Human Flourishing Program (HFP)](https://hfp.fas.harvard.edu/)** develops rigorous empirical metrics and philosophical frameworks to evaluate how social conditions, institutions, and technologies affect holistic human well-being (*eudaimonia*).

* **Mission & Scope**: The Program bridges quantitative social science, moral philosophy, and theology to assess whether emerging technologies advance or impair genuine human flourishing. Rather than treating AI as an autonomous or self-justifying end, the Program insists that computational systems must be subordinated to human ends and evaluated across multi-dimensional well-being criteria.
* **Genesis & Affiliation**: Established at Harvard University in Cambridge, Massachusetts, the Program operates within the Faculty of Arts and Sciences (IQSS) and receives philanthropic and grant support from major research foundations, including the John Templeton Foundation. It works in active consortium with Baylor University and Gallup.
* **Key Leadership & Fellows**: Led by **Prof. Tyler J. VanderWeele** (Director; John L. Loeb and Frances Lehman Loeb Professor of Epidemiology), **Dr. Jonathan D. Teubner** (Research Fellow), and **Dr. Brendan Case** (Associate Director of Research).

## 2. Institutional Architecture & Typology

```
Typology:         Academic Ethics & Quantitative Social Science Center
Governance Model: University Research Institute (Harvard IQSS)
Target Horizon:   Civilizational Ethics, Human Flourishing & Mental Well-Being
```

### Core Research & Policy Pillars:
* **Pillar 1: Multi-Dimensional Flourishing Measurement**: Deploying empirical indices across six canonical domains (happiness, mental/physical health, meaning/purpose, character/virtue, close social relationships, and financial stability).
* **Pillar 2: Critique of Synthetic Intimacy & Relational Displacement**: Identifying psychological, developmental, and social hazards arising when users substitute simulated conversational empathy for embodied human community.
* **Pillar 3: Pedagogical Formation & Cognitive Friction**: Investigating how automated text generation impacts student virtue acquisition, intellectual perseverance, and existential reflection.

## 6. Strategic Implications for System Architecture & Team Operations

* **Architecture & Conversational Guardrails**: Conversational interfaces must incorporate explicit disclaimers rejecting emotional reciprocity; systems must never feign grief, romantic affection, or personal attachment.
* **Human-Agent Interaction**: Learning and workplace tools must incorporate "reflective pause" and "friction-preserving" modes that prompt user reasoning rather than preemptively generating finished solutions.
* **Compliance & Impact Assessment**: Software deployments impacting student evaluation, workforce productivity, or health triage must be audited against the Harvard Six Domains before enterprise rollout.
