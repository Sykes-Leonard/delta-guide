# Harvard University AI Pedagogy & Socratic Learning: Derek Bok Center & CS50 Architecture

---

## 1. Executive Summary & Institutional Charter

At Harvard University, the pedagogical integration of artificial intelligence is guided by the **[Derek Bok Center for Teaching and Learning](https://bokcenter.harvard.edu/)**, the **Office of the Vice Provost for Advances in Learning (VPAL)**, and pioneering course implementations—most visibly **CS50** (Introduction to Computer Science), led by Gordon McKay Professor of the Practice of Computer Science **David J. Malan**.

* **Mission & Scope**: Rather than treating generative AI as a tool to be banned or a shortcut for homework completion, Harvard's educational leadership emphasizes **pedagogical AI engineering** and **Socratic scaffolding**. The university's core objective is to harness AI to provide individualized, 1-on-1 Socratic tutoring while intentionally preserving the "productive struggle" required for deep cognitive mastery.
* **Genesis & Affiliation**: Housed within Harvard University’s Faculty of Arts and Sciences in Cambridge, Massachusetts, the Bok Center collaborates with departments across Harvard College and graduate schools to redesign curricula, assessments, and classroom dynamics.
* **Key Leadership**: Coordinated through **Prof. David J. Malan** (Creator of *CS50* and *cs50.ai*), the **Derek Bok Center leadership**, and the **Berkman Klein Center for Internet & Society**.

### Core Pedagogical Pillars:
* **Pillar 1: Architecturally Socratic AI (*cs50.ai*)**: Customizing system prompts and inference guardrails so pedagogical bots strictly refuse to generate answers, full code, or completed essays. Instead, bots provide diagnostic questions, Socratic hints, and conceptual scaffolding.
* **Pillar 2: AI-Resistant & Process-Based Assessment**: Redesigning assignments to evaluate real-time problem-solving, draft iterations, in-class defenses, and oral explanations rather than static take-home submissions.
* **Pillar 3: The "Intellectual Sparring Partner" Model**: Teaching students to treat AI as a harsh critic, debate opponent, or testing sandbox rather than an automated authority.

## 6. Strategic Implications for System Architecture & High School AI Initiatives

For educational software development and secondary school AI programs (e.g. St. Francis High School):

1. **System Prompt Constraint Architecture**:
   Student-facing AI assistants must be hard-coded with Socratic guardrails modeled on *cs50.ai*:
   ```text
   You are a Socratic tutor. You must NEVER give the student the direct answer, complete code,
   or write text for them. If the student asks for a solution, analyze where their logic is
   breaking down and ask a targeted guiding question that helps them discover the fix themselves.
   ```
2. **Assessment Redesign**: Follow the Bok Center's guidance by evaluating the *process* of problem-solving—requiring students to submit reflective journals or oral walk-throughs alongside completed projects.
3. **Open Access Tiering**: Ensure that pedagogical tools do not create an equity divide between students who can afford premium LLM subscriptions and those who cannot.
