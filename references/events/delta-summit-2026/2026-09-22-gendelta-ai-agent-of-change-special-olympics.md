# GenDELTA: AI as an Agent of Change (Special Olympics International)

Presented by **Nathaniel Cook** (Chief Information and Technology Officer) and **Conal Fagan** (Senior Manager for Global Strategic Operations; Notre Dame '21) of **Special Olympics International**, on Tuesday, September 22, 2026, at the inaugural DELTA Summit on AI, Faith, and Human Flourishing.

---

## 1. Executive Summary & Core Theses

Representing Special Olympics International—a global movement supporting over 4 million athletes with intellectual disabilities across 190+ countries—Nathaniel Cook and Conal Fagan addressed the GenDELTA assembly on how emerging technologies can either reinforce historical exclusion or become catalytic agents of human flourishing:

1. **Inclusion Must Precede Optimization**: The primary danger of automated algorithmic systems is the systematic erasure of neurodivergent and intellectually disabled individuals from datasets. AI trained solely on "average" human performance will inevitably marginalize those who communicate, move, or learn differently. True AI alignment requires proactive, intentional inclusion of persons with disabilities at the foundational architectural layer.
2. **"AI-Shaped Problems" vs. Technology Shoehorning**:
   * Organizations often attempt to force generative AI into operational workflows where it does not belong (*"like trying to hammer a nail with a screwdriver"*).
   * Cook introduces the doctrine of **"AI-shaped problems"**: identifying precise bottlenecks centered on confirming, translating, processing, and predicting information (e.g. volunteer matching, multilingual dialect translation, and health screening coordination).
3. **Bridging the Global Digital Divide**:
   * AI cannot foster human flourishing if the physical rails of connectivity are absent. In nations across the Global South (e.g. Burkina Faso), the primary barrier to participation is lack of internet-connected mobile hardware. Special Olympics pairs AI deployments with hardware distribution and digital literacy programs.
4. **St. John Henry Newman's Call to Definite Service**:
   * Fagan concludes by grounding GenDELTA's youth leadership in St. John Henry Newman’s famous meditation on divine vocation: *"God has created me to do Him some definite service; He has committed some work to me which He has not committed to another."* Emerging leaders are commissioned to bring human dignity into every boardroom, engineering sprint, and classroom.

## 4. Key Takeaways & Practical Insights

### 4.1. The Doctrine of "AI-Shaped Problems"
Nathaniel Cook provides actionable counsel for non-profits, schools, and mission-driven institutions facing AI hype:
> *"Find AI-shaped problems. Don't try and make AI fit a problem you have. What I mean by that is there are problems that AI fits naturally into and solves without you having to contort and twist and force. Those are around confirming, changing, addressing, and predicting information. Focus on those spaces and you will have faster, more immediate, more direct success with AI, and you will not be fighting all the time to do it."* `[47:02]`

Applying AI where human relationship, pastoral care, or athletic coaching is required degrades the experience; applying AI to administrative logistics (e.g. donor/volunteer predictive matching) liberates humans to spend more time on the field with athletes.

### 4.2. Overcoming the Coach-to-Athlete Bottleneck
Special Olympics operates under an uncompromising safety ratio: **1 coach for every 12 athletes**. Scaling the mission to reach the next 100,000 athletes requires recruiting over 8,000 new volunteer coaches. 
* By analyzing organizational engagement data, AI identifies potential coaching candidates within the existing universe of volunteers, donors, and family members.
* Automating this administrative funnel directly expands the physical capacity of athletes to take the field.

### 4.3. Challenging Global Cultural Stigmas
Conal Fagan highlights that while Special Olympics has strong brand recognition in North America, in many regions around the world, intellectual disabilities are still shrouded in cultural shame or viewed as a curse:
* When athletes gather on the field of play, Special Olympics provides concurrent free health screenings (vision, dental, physical therapy) for individuals often completely excluded from national healthcare systems.
* AI-powered localized video translation and storyboarding help shift public narratives, modeling for local governments the inherent dignity and leadership capability of people with disabilities.

## 5. Verbatim High-Value Excerpts

### On the Inevitability of AI and Moral Responsibility
> `[46:15]` *"I think it's inevitable. And so I don't know that the pro/con evaluation is necessarily the right question to ask. If it weren't inevitable, I would be asking that question. Because I believe it is inevitable, we have to make sure the pros outweigh the cons... I don't know that that's organically going to happen if we don't put effort into it. We have to set out to make the change that allows it to be positive."*

### On the Digital Divide as the True Barrier
> `[43:33]` *"When was the last time anyone in this room went anywhere and did anything without using a phone or a computer to coordinate, plan, get directions, or get an Uber? That digital divide is actually a really big barrier, not just to participation in Special Olympics, but participation in the world as we know it today."*

### Conal Fagan on St. John Henry Newman and Calling
> `[48:43]` *"St. John Henry Newman wrote about his call to service. God has called you to some sort of service in your life. Whatever that is, how AI applies in that... just grapple with it. Keep trying, be resilient in it, knowing that we always have that higher calling. If you can bring a sense of dignity into every single space, if you can uplift the lives of persons with disability or other individuals who have been marginalized throughout history... ask the tough questions."*
